The Reagan Memo — Declassified 2027, Cascalifornia National Archives

"Control of water access provides leverage over population distribution and political behavior. A thirsty population is a manageable population."

Ronald Reagan, Governor of California, ordered the spigot to near-closure in his final year of office, 1974-1975. The decision was framed internally as "resource management." The classified memo, recovered from a federal annex during Cascalifornia's 2027 National Archives declassification, describes the actual rationale explicitly.

In 1978-1979, Reagan's pre-presidential political network arranged for the aqueduct to be removed from California school textbooks. Oregon and federal educational materials were similarly revised under the classification order. Every California child who went to school between 1979 and Cascalifornia's founding learned nothing about the aqueduct. Every drought they watched unfold was partially manufactured.

Reagan's surviving supporters describe this as "protecting California by managing expectations." Everyone else has been very loud about it since the memo's release.

Ted Wheeler — The Mayor of the Trail

Former Mayor of Portland · Pacific Crest Trail · Red Robe · Six-Foot Staff · Goats

Ted Wheeler served as Mayor of Portland through some of the most chaotic years in the city's recent history. He was tear-gassed at his own protest. He presided over a city that simultaneously voted to defund the police and voted him out for not defunding the police fast enough. His approval rating at the end of his tenure was a number best left unexamined.

Shortly after leaving office, something clicked for him. He acquired a red robe — not a hiking robe, not an outdoor technical garment, but a robe, the kind associated with medieval monasteries or the more serious kind of wizard — and a six-foot hardwood staff that he uses exclusively as a walking implement, never for anything else. He began walking the Pacific Crest Trail northbound from the Mexican border.

The goats were not planned. They appeared, one by one, over the first three weeks. By the Sierra Nevada he had seven goats. By Oregon he had eleven. They follow him without instruction. They are calm. They are loyal in a way that goats — which are famously, constitutionally disloyal — simply are not supposed to be. No explanation has been offered. None has been requested.

Trail Name: The Mayor

Assigned by the PCT hiking community without discussion. Wheeler accepted it without comment. He does not discuss Portland. He discusses the goats extensively. Each goat has a documented personality he can describe at length. The goats do not discuss Portland either.
