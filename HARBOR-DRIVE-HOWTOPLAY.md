# Harbor Drive: A Portland Saga
## How to Play & Outcomes Guide

---

## Overview

**Harbor Drive: A Portland Saga** is a branching narrative game set in 1974 Portland, Oregon. You play as the newly appointed City Commissioner faced with a series of infrastructure decisions that will define Portland's future — or destroy it, depending on your choices.

The game has **31 scenes** and **17 distinct endings**. Decisions compound. Earlier choices determine which later decisions are available to you. The lazy river situation in particular requires careful attention.

This guide contains **full spoilers**. If you want to discover the outcomes yourself, play first.

---

## The Factions

Four factions monitor your decisions throughout the game and appear via alert boxes at key moments. Their messages reflect their interests and shift in tone based on your choices.

### 🎭 Dante's Underground Council
The shadowy organization behind Portland's nightclub scene. Operators of Dante's Nightclub and its famous "Keep Portland Weird" sign. They want freeway traffic — cars mean customers. They are suspicious of parks, hostile to cyclists, and baffled by lazy rivers (though they eventually adapt to everything). Do not ignore their warnings.

### 📚 Powell's Books
Portland's legendary independent bookstore, here reimagined as a civic faction with strong opinions about waterfront foot traffic and pedestrian infrastructure. Powell's benefits from parks, plazas, boulevards, and bike lanes. They suffer when freeways dominate. They will sponsor almost anything if it brings people within walking distance of their doors.

### 🍩 Voodoo Donuts Syndicate
Chaotic good. Voodoo Donuts will open a location in, on, or adjacent to anything — lazy rivers, freeway ramps, underwater tunnels, bike paths, airport departure lounges. They are not a stabilizing force. They are a force of nature.

### 🍳 Screen Door Collective
Portland's beloved Southern comfort restaurant, famous for its wait times. Screen Door appears when neighborhood character is at stake — either thriving (boulevard conversions, waterfront parks) or suffering (freeway construction, air quality collapse). They adapt. They always find a way to seat you eventually.

## The Stats Bar

Four metrics are tracked throughout the game and update with each decision:

| Stat | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| **Political Capital** | Your standing with the public and city council. High = mandate. Zero = removal from office. |
| **Budget Health** | The city's financial position. Lazy rivers are expensive. Multiple lazy rivers are catastrophic. |
| **Weirdness** | Portland's commitment to its identity. Falls when you make boring, sensible choices. Rises with chaos. |
| **Dante's Favor** | The Underground Council's approval rating. High = they leave you alone. Low = watch your back. |

Stats are tracked for atmosphere and do not gate decisions — but they tell a story. Watch what happens to Budget Health when you start building lazy rivers.

## The Lazy River Rule

This is important. Read it.

**Lazy rivers are numbered in the order they are built.** The volcanic apocalypse is triggered only when **three lazy rivers are constructed**. Building two lazy rivers is strange and expensive but survivable. Building three destabilizes the volcanic system beneath Mount Hood.

| Lazy River | Default Assignment |
|---|---|
| **#1** | Harbor Drive waterfront (if chosen at the intro) OR 82nd Street to PDX (if chosen from the Mount Hood Freeway path) |
| **#2** | The second waterway built, depending on path |
| **#3** | The third — triggers volcanic apocalypse |

If you choose the park instead of a lazy river at Harbor Drive, and later build a lazy river to PDX, that becomes Lazy River #1 and the Great Loop becomes possible without volcanic risk.

## Decision Tree Overview

### The Opening Decision

At the start, you choose what to do with Harbor Drive:

```
Harbor Drive
├── Build Tom McCall Waterfront Park → [Park Path]
├── Keep Harbor Drive as a Freeway → [Freeway Ending]
└── Replace Harbor Drive with a Lazy River → [Lazy River Path]
```

### The Park Path

You remove Harbor Drive and build Tom McCall Waterfront Park. Powell's is thrilled. Dante's is not.

**Next decision:** What replaces the lost traffic capacity?

```
Park Path
├── Build Protected Bike Lanes → [Bike Lane Branch]
│   ├── Add Bikes to the Banfield → [Absurd Harmony Ending]
│   └── No Bikes on the Banfield → [Reasonable Ending]
├── Build the Mount Hood Freeway (Division/Powell eastbound) → [MHF Branch]
│   ├── I-205 via 52nd Street → [Burnside decision] → 2 endings
│   ├── I-205 via 92nd Street → [Burnside decision] → 2 endings
│   ├── No I-205: 82nd Street Parkway to PDX → [Visionary Ending]
│   ├── Lazy River to PDX (First River) → [Great Loop Ending]
│   ├── Lazy River to PDX + Keep Building → [2nd River Branch]
│   │   ├── Stop at two → [Great Loop Ending]
│   │   └── Convert Banfield (3rd River) → [Volcanic Apocalypse]
│   └── Build ALL Unbuilt Freeways → [Concrete Ending / LA Exodus]
└── Build a Streetcar Network → [Civilized / Boring Ending]
```

---

### The Lazy River Path

You replace Harbor Drive with Lazy River #1. Voodoo Donuts immediately announces underwater kiosk plans.

**Next decision:** What do you do about the Banfield and lost traffic capacity?

```
Lazy River Path (Harbor Drive = River #1)
├── Mount Hood Freeway + Keep Banfield → [Burnside Decision]
│   ├── Approve Burnside Bike Lane → [I-205 Decision]
│   │   ├── I-205 via 52nd → 2 endings
│   │   ├── I-205 via 92nd → 2 endings  
│   │   ├── Parkway to PDX → [Visionary Ending]
│   │   ├── Lazy River #2 to PDX → [Great Loop or River #3]
│   │   └── All Freeways → [Concrete Ending / LA Exodus]
│   └── Reject Burnside Bike Lane → [same I-205 decisions above]
├── Mount Hood Freeway + Banfield = River #2 → [I-205 Decision]
│   └── River #3 option available → [Volcanic Apocalypse]
├── Mount Hood Freeway + Banfield = Boulevard → [Burnside Decision] → 2 endings
├── Mount Hood Freeway + Banfield = America's Widest Bike Path → [Glorious Absurdity Ending]
├── Banfield = River #2 (No Mount Hood Freeway) → [Bankruptcy / Tualatin Ending]
└── Everything at Once (2 Rivers + MHF + Keep Banfield) → [Division Street Decision]
    ├── Accept Division St. as Bike Territory → [Anarchist Ending]
    └── Send Police → [Bike Wars Ending]
```

## All Endings

### 🌊 THE GREAT LOOP ENDING
**How to reach it:** Build the Mount Hood Freeway. Build a lazy river to PDX (as first or second lazy river). Choose to complete the Milwaukie Extension and airport bypass canal.

**What happens:** Portland builds a complete inner-tube circuit using the 82nd Street Lazy River, a bypass canal around PDX's north boundary to the Columbia River, the Columbia itself flowing west, the Willamette through downtown, and a Milwaukie Extension connecting back to 82nd. Neighborhood canals spread organically through Southeast and Northeast Portland. Dante's installs a floating dock on the Willamette. Voodoo Donuts operates the PDX departure lounge. Your statue stands knee-deep in water at the Milwaukie confluence, holding an inner tube.

**Verdict:** The best ending. Genuinely wonderful. Portland becomes a water city by accident.

---

### 🏆 THE PERFECT ENDING
**How to reach it:** Build Tom McCall Waterfront Park. Build the Mount Hood Freeway. Approve the Burnside bike lane. Choose I-205 wisely.

**What happens:** All systems work in harmony. The waterfront thrives. The freeway moves traffic east. Burnside serves cyclists. Portland achieves impossible balance. Dante's declares you "acceptable."

**Verdict:** Sensible and satisfying.

---

### 🌋 THE VOLCANIC APOCALYPSE
**How to reach it:** Build three lazy rivers. Any three. The specific combination does not matter — three lazy rivers is three too many.

**What happens:** The massive volume of water pumped into the ground for the third lazy river destabilizes Mount Hood's volcanic system. The mountain erupts for the first time in 400 years. Lahars flow. Portland is buried in ash. 2 million people evacuate. Dante's is entombed. Powell's is destroyed. Voodoo Donuts is vaporized. The city is uninhabitable. You are remembered forever as the Commissioner Who Built Three Lazy Rivers.

**Verdict:** You did this to yourself.

---

### 💸 THE BANKRUPTCY ENDING — Tualatin Valley Exodus
**How to reach it:** Replace Harbor Drive with a lazy river AND replace the Banfield with a lazy river, with no Mount Hood Freeway to compensate.

**What happens:** Two lazy rivers with no replacement traffic capacity bankrupts the city. 250,000 residents relocate to the Tualatin Valley — Beaverton, Tigard, Sherwood. Portland slowly sinks into metaphorical irrelevance. Your statue is erected in a Tualatin Valley parking lot as a warning. Powell's opens in Beaverton. Voodoo Donuts follows.

**Verdict:** You overcommitted to water.

---

### 🏗️ THE CONCRETE ENDING — The LA Exodus
**How to reach it:** Choose to build ALL the unbuilt freeways simultaneously.

**What happens:** The Rose City Freeway (northeast to Troutdale), Northwest Industrial Freeway (US 30 upgraded west toward Astoria), State Route 217 Extension (tunnel under Forest Park to St. Johns), and dual I-205 loops (52nd AND 92nd) are all constructed. Portland's air quality collapses permanently. Forest Park dies from emissions seeping through the 217 tunnel. Children develop asthma. Mount Hood disappears behind a permanent gray haze.

Mass exodus occurs — to Los Angeles. Because Los Angeles, despite its reputation, actually rejected dozens of freeway proposals: the Whitnall Freeway, Reseda-to-the-Sea, the original Santa Monica Freeway routing through Beverly Hills, the Harbor/Downtown Split, the Industrial Freeway, the Rosemead Freeway, the Huntington Beach Freeway, the Marina/Slauson Freeway, the Long Beach Extension to Pasadena, most of the Pacific Coast Freeway, and others. Portland built more freeways than LA. Portlanders flee to LA for fresher air.

**Verdict:** You out-freewayed Los Angeles. This is the worst possible outcome. Your statue is in a highway median, slowly being poisoned by diesel fumes.

---

### 🎭 DANTE'S TAKES OVER
**How to reach it:** Keep Harbor Drive as a freeway.

**What happens:** You vote to keep the freeway. Dante's approves. Powell's is furious and withdraws funding. The waterfront never recovers. Years later, Dante's Underground Council removes you from office on fabricated charges. They control Portland's infrastructure for 20 years. The city's weirdness plummets. Voodoo Donuts becomes the de facto government.

**Verdict:** You chose the freeway and got exactly what you deserved.

---

### 🚴 THE ANARCHIST ENDING — Power to the Pedals
**How to reach it:** Build two lazy rivers and the Mount Hood Freeway simultaneously, then accept Division Street as permanent bike territory.

**What happens:** By legitimizing cyclist direct action, you create a new form of Portland governance. Cyclists claim three more streets within a month. By year three, bikes outnumber cars. The Mount Hood Freeway is abandoned. The lazy rivers serve all transportation needs. Dante's loses its car traffic but discovers cyclists party harder than expected. No government remains. Just rivers, bicycles, and donuts.

**Verdict:** Chaotic good. Portland is weird. This is fine.

---

### 🚨 THE BIKE WARS ENDING
**How to reach it:** Build two lazy rivers and the Mount Hood Freeway, then send police to clear Division Street cyclists.

**What happens:** The largest bike-based civil disobedience event in North American history. Thousands of fixie riders. National Guard deployed. No weapons — just bikes and determination. Nobody dies. Everyone is vegan. Portland's reputation as America's weirdest city is permanently cemented. You are removed from office. Dante's installs martial law (bike-based). Portland becomes a sociology textbook.

**Verdict:** You escalated unnecessarily. The cyclists always win.

---

### 🚲 THE 40-YEAR CYCLIST REVENGE
**How to reach it:** Build the lazy river and Mount Hood Freeway, then reject the Burnside bike lane.

**What happens:** Cyclists infiltrate Portland's government over 40 years. By 2015, Burnside is a bike lane anyway — without your help. The Portland Cycling Coalition overthrows Dante's Underground Council. You are completely forgotten. The cyclists install a statue of you being crushed by a fixed-gear bike.

**Verdict:** They had long memories. You should have approved the lane.

---

### 🎵 THE GLORIOUS ABSURDITY ENDING
**How to reach it:** Build the Mount Hood Freeway, then convert the entire Banfield Expressway to America's Widest Bike Path.

**What happens:** All six-plus lanes of I-84, the shoulders, the median — everything — is converted to protected cycling infrastructure. A velodrome from the Rose Quarter to Troutdale. Voodoo Donuts installs bike-through windows every half mile. Powell's opens locations at every former on-ramp. Dante's nightclubs along the path become legendary, accessible only by bicycle, open until 2 AM. Civil engineers worldwide study it in disbelief. It works.

**Verdict:** Absurd. Also correct.

---

### 🌲 THE VISIONARY ENDING
**How to reach it:** Choose no I-205 at all — build an 82nd Street Parkway to PDX instead.

**What happens:** Portland rejects the federal freeway mandate. 82nd Street becomes a tree-lined parkway with a grand ceremonial entrance to Portland International Airport. The city grows up instead of out. Density increases. The airport entrance becomes internationally recognized. You are remembered as the Commissioner who broke the freeway cycle.

**Verdict:** Genuinely the most historically informed choice. Portland did eventually reject further freeway expansion.

---

### 🚋 THE CIVILIZED / BORING ENDING
**How to reach it:** Build the park, then choose a streetcar network.

**What happens:** The streetcar works beautifully. Portland becomes more European. More civilized. Less weird. Hipsters from Brooklyn eventually move in because it becomes normal. Portland is no longer maximally weird. This is, quietly, the worst ending that doesn't involve volcanoes or concrete.

**Verdict:** You made Portland boring. Congratulations on your functional transit system.

---

### The I-205 Family of Endings

There are four additional endings based on I-205 corridor choice combined with the Burnside bike lane decision. They represent the more realistic, complicated outcomes where Portland builds infrastructure that is neither utopian nor catastrophic — just the complicated, human mess of real urban planning.

| Path | Bike Lane | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| I-205 via 52nd | Approved | Progress at human cost — neighborhoods split, but cyclists thrive |
| I-205 via 52nd | Rejected | Practical, uninspired — your statue is in a traffic median |
| I-205 via 92nd | Approved | Suburban expansion with cycling — Gresham booms, Burnside bikes |
| I-205 via 92nd | Rejected | Maximum sprawl — your statue is in a Gresham parking lot |

## Tips

1. **Three lazy rivers = volcano.** This is not negotiable. Mount Hood does not care about your transportation vision.
2. **Dante's Favor matters more than it looks.** Keep it above zero unless you enjoy being removed from office.
3. **The Great Loop is the best outcome.** It requires patience: build the Mount Hood Freeway, build a lazy river to PDX, then fund the Milwaukie Extension and airport bypass canal.
4. **The Concrete Ending is a trap.** Building all the freeways feels powerful. It ends with people moving to Los Angeles for fresh air.
5. **Cyclists always win eventually.** The only question is how long it takes and how much they respect you when it happens.
6. **Powell's is always watching.** Decisions that increase pedestrian traffic near water make them happy. Decisions that create freeway noise make them sad. Act accordingly.
